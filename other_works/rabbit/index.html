Rabbit is a marketplace for pre-loved condos at fair prices, made for young adults buying their first home. The brief was an iOS app mockup with a logo and the full app flow, using the general pages of shopping apps. I went further and built it as a working, clickable prototype that runs in the browser and installs on a phone like a native app.

Problem Statement

Buying a first home is the biggest purchase most people make, and the tools around it feel built for experts. Listings hide the real monthly cost, the condition of an older condo is a guess until late in the process, and making an offer means paperwork, jargon and long silences. For a first-time buyer, every step feels like a risk.

Goals

  • Make browsing a condo feel as easy and familiar as shopping in a well-designed store app.
  • Put the honest numbers first: the monthly cost, what the HOA fee covers and the condition of the home.
  • Turn making an offer into a guided flow, from the first tour through to closing.
  • Create a brand, logo and palette that feel friendly and trustworthy to a young audience.
  • Deliver something people can actually tap through and share, not just static screens.

A Store, Not a Spreadsheet

The brief pointed to the Apple Store iOS app for inspiration. I kept its structure, which people already know how to use, and gave it Rabbit’s own brand and palette.

  • For You: top matches for your budget and neighbourhoods, price drops, active offers and upcoming tours, plus short Buyer School guides.
  • Browse: condos by category (studios, one bedroom, under $200k, pet friendly) and by neighbourhood.
  • Search: live text search, bedroom chips, a filter sheet and recent searches.
  • Saved: a shortlist with a side-by-side compare, and Account for offers, tours, messages and preferences.

Every Home Inspected

  • Price you can plan around: every listing shows an estimated monthly cost next to the price, and a breakdown of mortgage, HOA fee and property tax.
  • Rabbit Inspected: each condo has a condition score and an inspection report, item by item, so the age of a home is something you can see rather than fear.
  • Building facts: HOA reserves, transit, parking and a map, plus the listing agent and similar condos.
  • Book a tour: in person or by video call, with a day and time picker. Booked tours appear on the listing and on For You.

Making an Offer, Like Checking Out

  • Offer configurator: choose an amount (asking, 2% or 5% under, or your own), down payment, loan term, move-in date and contingencies. The monthly estimate updates as you go.
  • Review & pay: a bag-style summary with a $500 good-faith deposit, clearly marked as refundable.
  • Offer tracker: a timeline from submitted to closing. The seller can accept or counter, and you can accept, decline or withdraw. A demo button skips ahead, so anyone can see the whole flow in a minute.

Saved, Chat and Account

  • Chat with the listing agent, with quick replies for common questions.
  • A four-step onboarding (name, budget, bedrooms, neighbourhoods and must-haves) that shapes For You from the first launch.
  • Everything you do (saves, tours, offers and chats) is remembered in the browser, so the prototype behaves like a real app between visits.

Brand

  • Logo: a house with rabbit ears, window eyes and a door, on a plum tile, with a lowercase “rabbit” wordmark. Friendly, simple, and it reads at app-icon size.
  • Palette: Plum and Slate for the brand, Paper for surfaces, and Deep Teal for primary actions, with lighter Teal for fills and highlights. A slightly darker teal keeps teal text readable.
  • Type: Bricolage Grotesque for headlines, with a bit of character, and Figtree for everything you read.

Built to Be Shared

I designed Rabbit and built it with Claude Code, as a real front end rather than a set of linked screens.

  • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no build step or dependencies.
  • A small router with push and pop transitions, bottom sheets and toasts, so it moves like an iOS app.
  • Runs full screen on a phone and can be added to the home screen. On a laptop, reviewers can jump straight to any of 14 screens.
  • Listings, agents and guides live in one data file, and mortgage maths runs on example rates.

Outcome & Learnings

  • Borrowing the structure of a familiar shopping app made a complex purchase feel approachable.
  • Showing the monthly cost and the inspection up front answered the two questions first-time buyers worry about most.
  • A clickable prototype says far more than static mockups. Reviewers can make an offer, get a counter and close the deal themselves.
  • Listings, prices, agents and inspections are sample content. No offers are sent and no money moves.
